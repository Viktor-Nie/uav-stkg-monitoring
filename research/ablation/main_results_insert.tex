\paragraph{Controlled language results}
In condition order full, without role mapping, without skeleton, and without both, mean scope macro F1 is 0.987, 0.974, 0.974, and 0.987, and mean tuple exactness is 0.989, 0.979, 0.979, and 0.989. Across five contexts, each scope F1 ranges from 0.936 to 1.000 and tuple exactness from 18/19 to 19/19. Role-set agreement is 5/5 in every run. The small differences do not establish a role-mapping advantage; when a skeleton is retained, it still carries recoverable role information. After source correction, no positive bounded-response item remains, so response-role effectiveness is not evaluated.

With skeletons, all twenty-five reference-positive descriptor opportunities per condition pass the gate, together with one and two false acceptances of the groundspeed item in the full and mapping-removed conditions. Without skeletons, none of the positive descriptors is admitted: formula fields differ from the strict canonical serialization even though scope--signal--template agreement remains high. Mean formula-structure exactness is 0.989, 0.979, 0.716, and 0.726 respectively. Thus the filtering behavior concerns representation compatibility rather than a correction of all semantic errors. The historical typed output, rescored without regeneration, matches 18/19 tuples on the reviewed reference while retaining its original 17/19 score on the historical reference.

All twenty-nine catalog-consistent challenge candidates pass the structural gate: five matched controls and twenty-four semantically invalid candidates. The gate therefore does not certify that an otherwise consistent descriptor means what its source sentence says. This constructed challenge and the twenty-run ablation are reported separately from the 354 structural graph mutants. The explicit-value guard passes all twenty-four original internal texts unchanged and refuses conflicting numerical text; its limited grammar is a conservative control, not physical threshold calibration or a general numerical-language parser.
